## Chosen Direction: Forest Systems

### Design Movement
Contemporary editorial brutalism softened by product-design clarity: the confidence of a technical portfolio with the warmth of a studio identity.

### Core Principles
1. **Signal over decoration:** Orange is reserved for decisions, links, and moments of emphasis.
2. **Structured asymmetry:** Layouts use offset columns, vertical labels, and anchored utility panels rather than centered generic stacks.
3. **Quiet depth:** Forest surfaces, hairline borders, glow fields, and grain create depth without visual noise.
4. **Proof through craft:** Every section should make technical capability tangible through project artifacts, metrics, systems language, and precise microcopy.

### Color Philosophy
Deep forest green is the trust layer: it feels grounded, intelligent, and human. Near-black gives the work room to breathe. Orange behaves like a live system signal—small, confident, and impossible to miss. Emerald reinforces continuity and technical fluency without competing with the signature orange.

### Layout Paradigm
A wide, framed canvas with a sticky utility header and asymmetrical content lanes. The hero uses a three-part composition: narrative left, portrait/visual center, capability rail right. Lower sections shift between offset text blocks, horizontally scrolling skill strips, and large project surfaces.

### Signature Elements
- Thin orange measurement rules and section index labels.
- A soft forest “orbital” field behind portraits and project media.
- Compact status chips that read like system telemetry: `AVAILABLE`, `BUILDING`, `01—05`.

### Interaction Philosophy
Interactions should feel like inspecting a live system: focus states are visible, hover states expose depth, links respond with short directional motion, and actions always have a clear outcome. Navigation is direct and anchor-based; mobile navigation becomes a focused drawer rather than a compressed desktop bar.

### Animation
Use 180–260ms ease-out transitions for hover, focus, and button feedback. Reveal section content with small upward translation and opacity only when motion is allowed. Let orbital background elements drift slowly, but keep text and primary actions still. Respect `prefers-reduced-motion` by removing non-essential transforms and looping effects.

### Typography System
Display: Space Grotesk, 700–600, with tight tracking for the hero and section headlines. Body: Inter, 400–600, with generous line-height for explanatory copy. Metadata: Inter, 600, uppercase, 0.14em tracking. Headings should alternate between compact technical labels and broad editorial statements.

### Brand Essence
Munyoh Joel Tamon builds intelligent software for teams turning ambitious ideas into useful systems. Personality: **precise, curious, dependable**.

### Brand Voice
Headlines are direct and confident. CTAs are specific and action-oriented. Microcopy sounds like a thoughtful engineer explaining what matters, never like generic startup filler.

Example lines:
- “I turn complex systems into clear, useful experiences.”
- “Have a hard problem? Let’s map the first move.”

### Wordmark & Logo
A compact `J/` monogram made from a vertical stem and a diagonal signal slash, paired with the wordmark `JOEL.` in Space Grotesk. The slash becomes the visual cue for navigation, separators, and favicon usage.

### Signature Brand Color
Signal Orange `#FF6B35` — a warm, ownable orange that communicates movement and decision-making against the forest field.

## Style Decisions

- Signal Orange `#FF6B35` appears primarily on actions, section indices, key numerals, and decisive phrases; quieter technical information uses emerald, forest tones, hairlines, and type hierarchy.
- Project surfaces lead with concrete artifact labels and product visuals before descriptive copy.
- Capability cards show tools-in-use and evidence signals rather than arbitrary proficiency percentages.
- The `J/` slash mark repeats as a section cue to make the identity more ownable across the page.
